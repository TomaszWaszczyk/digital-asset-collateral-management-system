# Digital Asset Collateral Management System

When a bank lends against Bitcoin without ever holding it, the credit system changes shape.

> Jeonbuk Bank, a regional lender in South Korea, has made a corporate loan in won secured by Bitcoin. The coins stay with a licensed third-party custodian. The bank has neither ownership nor direct control, including if the borrower defaults. Unchained, via Yahoo Finance (12 Oct 2026).

Three things shift in the architecture:

- Custody leaves the bank. Traditional collateral sits where the lender can reach it. Here the asset stays outside the balance sheet, with a virtual-asset service provider registered under Korea’s financial reporting law. Credit is extended; the coins are not taken in.

- Default becomes a court sale, not a seizure. If the borrower fails, the bank does not receive Bitcoin. It asks a court to liquidate the collateral and repays itself from the cash. That path exists only because Korea’s amended Civil Enforcement Rules, in force since 1 October, now cover seizing and selling virtual assets. The product sits on a change in civil procedure.

- The old lending stack stays. The bank values the pledge with its own multi-exchange price, stress-tested for a sharp drop, and books the loan through systems built for ordinary collateral. No crypto treasury, no new asset class on the books. The design is meant to fit the Banking Act without a regulatory sandbox.

The loan is corporate-only, Bitcoin-only, and limited to operating funds. Size, rate, and loan-to-value were not disclosed.

The structural point: Bitcoin enters bank credit as a claim enforced by a court and a custodian, not as an asset the bank holds.
